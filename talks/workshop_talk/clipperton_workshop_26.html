01 · Taylor Swift

Swiftian Saudade:
joy and sorrow
interwoven.

Taylor Swift’s lyrics analyzed with lexicon approaches

“You do not get to tell me about Sad”: Swiftian Saudade in Taylor Swift’s Lyrics (2026) · 423 tracks

~1 min. This builds on our published article on Swiftian saudade. Saudade is a Portuguese word for bittersweet longing — happiness and sadness held together. We operationalize it with the NRC emotion lexicon: a song is saudade when sadness is in its top three emotions together with joy or anticipation. The dataset covers 423 tracks across every album version, and I updated it the week The Life of a Showgirl came out.

01 · Taylor Swift

Showgirl is her most positive era.

Second only to TTPD in emotional focus.

~1.5 min. Now each song. Grey dots are songs; teal squares are album averages; orange is Showgirl. Showgirl’s average sits up and to the right: the most positive of any era, and second only to TTPD in emotional focus. It also has the most joy words per song of any era — 17.6 — yet 31 percent of its songs still meet the saudade definition. Inside the album the spread is wide: the title track and Ruin the Friendship sit far up top, Eldest Daughter (track five) is intensely emotional, and Patient Zero is the most negative song on the record.

02 · Campaign playlists

Do rally playlists
carry a message?

232 rally songs 13 candidates 2016–2024

~1 min. Second project: campaign music. Walk-on songs are a deliberate choice, so do they carry the party’s and candidate’s message? We built a dataset of 232 rally songs — 195 distinct — from 13 presidential candidates across 2016, 2020 and 2024. Same toolkit as the Swift work: LIWC and NRC scores, then PCA. Drawing on work on party asymmetry, we expected Republicans to compete on conservatism — religion and nostalgia — and Democrats to appeal to many groups, with candidates’ songs matching their personal brand: Trump and the past, Clinton and care, Biden and authenticity, Harris and joy.

02 · Campaign playlists

Country is Republican. R&B is Democratic.

Pop and rock are shared ground.

~45 sec. First, genre. Country is strongly Republican and R&B is strongly Democratic — both well beyond chance (chi-square 27, p under .001) — while pop and rock are shared ground. That fits Democrats assembling a coalition of groups and Republicans leaning into a more traditional identity.

02 · Campaigns

Republicans play the past.

Every GOP campaign sits above the Democratic average on nostalgia.

~1.5 min. Red triangles are Republican campaigns, blue circles Democratic; the large markers are party averages with bootstrap intervals. The vertical axis runs from forward-looking to golden-era language. Every Republican campaign sits above the Democratic average — Cruz 2016 and Trump 2024 most of all — consistent with Make America Great Again-style nostalgia. Democrats are more forward-looking and more spread out, fitting a party built from many groups. Which campaign a song belongs to explains about 15 percent of variation on this nostalgia dimension, and essentially none on collective-versus-individual language.
